Why the diagram in your wiki is wrong

It was accurate eighteen months ago. It is a PNG, so the one person who spotted the mistake could not fix it. Whoever drew it has left, and half the boxes have no owner any more.

Diagrams rot because editing them is somebody else's job. Text does not have that problem — a diagram in your repository gets reviewed like code, and anyone can correct a line. The catch has always been that the result looked like a draft.

What it does with your text

Six things happen between the line you type and the picture you show. Each one is worth a look, because each one is work you would otherwise do by hand in a drawing tool.

01 — Read

It infers what things are

A node called PostgreSQL becomes a database cylinder wearing the Postgres mark. Kafka becomes a queue. Anything ending external gets a dashed border. Nothing in the diagram opposite was annotated by hand.

Override any of it, and the file still renders in GitHub:

%%mp: node api archetype=service icon=aws:lambda

02 — Lay out

Groups nest, edges route, nothing overlaps

Subgraphs are real containers, not rectangles drawn behind things. Edges take orthogonal routes with reserved space for their labels. Five hundred nodes lay out in under a tenth of a second.

Collapse a group and the edges that crossed its boundary bundle into one, carrying a count — so a large system reads at the level you need. 

%%mp: group data collapsed

03 — Explain

Click anything and it tells you what it is

Selecting a component lights up everything it touches and explains it along the bottom: what depends on it, what it depends on, and whatever the author wrote as a note. The links are clickable, so you can walk the graph without reading the source.

Arrow keys do the same thing, following connections rather than the screen.

%%mp: node orders note="Owns the order lifecycle."
  owner="Commerce" sla="99.95%"

04 — Present

The diagram walks people through itself

Number a few steps and it becomes a presentation: each step frames what it names, dims the rest, and shows its note. One key drops every piece of chrome so the diagram takes the whole screen.

A highlight travels the edges in dependency order — an edge lights as the one feeding it lands — so the picture moves the way a request does. 

%%mp: step 1 title="A shopper arrives" focus=shopper,cdn

05 — Theme

Twenty themes, and the tokens behind them

Clean, Slate, Blueprint, Notebook, Vivid, Contrast, Paper, Mono, Terminal, Ocean — each in light and dark. Every text and fill pair is contrast-checked by a test, as is the canvas against the nodes standing on it.

Edit the tokens directly and export the result as JSON, or pin one to a document.

%%mp: theme slate-dark

Your architecture never leaves the browser

Parsing, layout, rendering and export all run on your machine. There is no account, no upload, and no server holding a copy of how your systems fit together — which is the part most diagram tools ask you to hand over.

It is not a promise in a policy document. It is a property of the build, and a test asserts it.
